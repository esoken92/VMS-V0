Initial Outline of what program should acheive

I would like to make a program that monitors the Wellington Group tyre flow through their retreading factory:
Ideally the program should be able to have the following layout:

    1. Inbound - Tyres that are en route to the factory and due for pickup. The date the request was made to collect them should be recorded for tracking.

    2. Landed - Tyres that have arrived at the factory and await their first inspection. They are typically stored in the warehouse and tagged for identification.

    3. First Inspection - The tyres undergo an initial quality check. At this stage, tyres may be rejected based on factors such as:
        - Excessive wear or damage
        - Structural deformities
        - Irregular tread patterns
        - Previous repairs affecting integrity
        - Other

    Rejected tyres should be tagged and routed to outbound with recorded rejection reasons.

    4. Buffing - The remaining tread is removed to prepare the casing for retreading. Possible faults that could lead to rejection include:
        - Uneven buffing
        - Excessive casing damage
        - Exposed cords or weak structural integrity
        - Other

    Any rejected tyres are marked and sent to outbound with documented reasons.

    5. Skiving - Defects, excess rubber, and impurities are removed from the tyre surface. Possible rejection reasons include:
        - Cracks or cuts in the rubber
        - Uneven surface or contamination
        - Air pockets or structural weaknesses
        - Other

    If necessary, rejected tyres are redirected to outbound.

    6. Cushioning - A fresh rubber layer is applied for smooth adhesion between the casing and the new tread. Rejection factors could include:
        - Poor bonding or adhesion issues
        - Presence of air bubbles or voids
        - Incorrect material application
        - Other

    Rejected tyres should be tagged and documented for outbound.

    7. Building - The tread is applied, securing the tyre's reconstructed structure. Possible reasons for rejection:
        - Misaligned tread application
        - Weak bonding or poor structural integrity
        - Material defects or inconsistencies
        - Other

    Rejected tyres must be recorded before being moved to outbound.

    8. Enveloping - The tyre is placed inside an envelope or curing chamber to prepare for vulcanization. Rejection causes may include:
        - Improper sealing leading to air leaks
        - Contaminated surfaces impacting adhesion
        - Incorrect placement or equipment failure
        - Other

    Rejected tyres should be tracked and sent to outbound.

    9. Curing - The tyre undergoes heat treatment to permanently bond the tread to the casing. Possible defects leading to rejection:
        - Uneven heat distribution
        - Improper curing temperature or timing
        - Warped structure or internal defects
        - Other

    Rejected tyres should be removed from production and documented.

    10. Final Inspection - A thorough check ensures the tyre meets safety and quality standards. Reasons for rejection could include:
        - Incorrect tread wear or pattern issues
        - Structural integrity failure
        - Surface inconsistencies or external damage
        - Other

    Failed tyres must be tagged and moved to outbound for disposal or return.

    11. Painting - A protective coat or marking is applied to the tyre. While rare, rejection could occur due to:
        - Uneven or incomplete paint coverage
        - Incorrect labeling or identification marks
        - Material contamination or surface defects
        - Other

    Any rejected tyres must be recorded and routed to outbound.

    12. Outbound - Rejected tyres are sent for disposal or return, while approved tyres are dispatched for delivery with proper inventory tracking.

Technical Considerations
    - All data should be secure and encryted when sending, receiving and storage. Set Firestore rules to limit document access by uid or role
    - RBAC should be in full effect with ESO team having full control.
    - No sensitive logic in frontend — all rejections, timestamps, and validations must be enforced on the server.
    - Use middleware on the backend to check roles (e.g., isAdmin, isManager)
    - Use role claims in JWT or Firebase custom claims
    - Frontend hides buttons/screens based on role
    - Use UUIDs or server-generated tyre IDs to prevent spoofing.
    - Disable browser dev tools in production (console.log stripping, no debug tools exposed).
    - Obfuscate production builds.
    Every user action (e.g., reject tyre, update status) should:
        - Be logged with timestamp
        - Include userId, IP, device type
        - Be viewable only by Admin or Auditor role
    Secure Communication:
        - HTTPS only — enforced on all endpoints
        - Use SSL pinning for mobile app if extremely sensitive
        - Rate-limit API calls to prevent brute-force attacks
    Security Testing:
        - OWASP ZAP for web app scanning
        - Mobile Security Framework (MobSF) for mobile app
